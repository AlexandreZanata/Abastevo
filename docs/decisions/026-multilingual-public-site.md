# ADR-026: Multilingual public site, pt-BR first

Accepted by the owner on 2026-11-18. This decision supersedes D-SEO-07 of the
earlier SEO growth plan ("pt-BR only; no hreflang until a real non-Portuguese
audience exists"). The rules and tasks are in the
[organic growth and multilingual contract](../product/landing/organic-growth-i18n-plan.md).

## Context

The public site (`landing/` and the static portal generated by `portalgen`)
serves only pt-BR. The Android app already ships 8 locales.

The owner wants more organic visitors and indexing in other languages. The
model is sorrifit.com, which publishes about 257 pages in each of 12 locales
with reciprocal hreflang.

Demand for Brazilian fuel prices is mostly Portuguese. Translating every
generated page would create thousands of near-duplicate pages, which is the
pattern that search-engine scaled-content policies target.

## Decision

1. **Locales.** pt-BR stays at the root with unchanged URLs. Wave A adds `en` and `es` under `/en/` and `/es/`, with translated section names and slugs. Other app locales open one at a time, only when the measurement rules in the contract show demand and a reviewer exists.
2. **What is translated:**
   - home, about and editorial policy;
   - the calculators;
   - an international guide set;
   - price hubs (national, fuel, state, city);
   - methodology, data and insights.

   **What stays pt-BR:** station pages, enforcement records and the forum. Legal pages get an informative `noindex` translation in which Portuguese prevails. App tutorials follow only with captures taken in the same locale.
3. **hreflang and canonicals.**
   - A page's hreflang list contains only the indexable members of its translation group, plus `x-default` pointing to the pt-BR page.
   - The canonical is always self.
   - The same indexation decision that drives robots and sitemaps also drives hreflang.
   - The portal splits its sitemaps per locale. The landing keeps one urlset.
4. **No automatic redirects.** No redirect by Accept-Language or IP, and no language cookie. The switcher is plain links. The cookie-free promise stays.
5. **Reviewed translations only.**
   - Each page starts as an AI draft and is reviewed by a fluent human against a shared glossary seeded from the app strings.
   - The build emits only reviewed pages.
   - A newer pt-BR source marks a translation stale. It stays `noindex` until it is reviewed again.
   - One locale registry in `contracts/i18n/` drives both generators.
6. **Prices and units.**
   - Prices stay in BRL per litre.
   - English pages may add BRL per gallon.
   - A US$ value waits for an admitted exchange-rate source.
7. **Authorship.**
   - A named human editor with a bio page, once the owner provides one.
   - Until then, "Equipe abastevo" with the editorial policy.
8. **Measurement.**
   - Search Console and Bing Webmaster.
   - Optional first-party, cookie-free aggregated access-log counts, with IP truncated and 30-day retention.
   - The log counts are enabled only together with the matching privacy policy update.

## Alternatives

- **Keep pt-BR only.** This forgoes visitors, cross-border drivers, researchers and journalists who search in English or Spanish.
- **Copy SorriFit's 12 locales for every page.** This multiplies thin station pages on a new domain and spreads crawl budget, with little matching demand.
- **Redirect by Accept-Language with a language cookie.** This breaks edge caching of the home and the no-cookie promise.

## Verification and follow-up

The locale tasks (SEO-25 to SEO-28) must prove the rules with build and
portal tests:
- reciprocity and `x-default`;
- no hreflang to a `noindex` or missing page;
- unreviewed pages never emitted;
- stale-translation gating;
- string parity across locales;
- locale-aware number parsing in the calculators.

The open work is listed in [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md) under
Web/landing/SEO.
