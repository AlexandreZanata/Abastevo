**Quick answer:** the number after the "S" indicates the **maximum sulfur
content**, in milligrams per kilogram: S10 has up to 10 mg/kg and S500, up to
500 mg/kg. Diesel vehicles with modern emission control systems, which in
general are those built from 2012 onward, left the factory designed for S10,
and using S500 in them can damage those systems. Older vehicles can use S10
without any problem. The rule that applies to your vehicle is in the
manufacturer's manual.
:::

## What the number means

In the diesel sold in Brazil, the letter **S** stands for sulfur and the
number indicates the maximum limit of that element in the fuel, measured in
milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg), a unit equivalent to parts per million:

- **Diesel S10:** up to 10 mg/kg of sulfur;
- **Diesel S500:** up to 500 mg/kg of sulfur.

Both follow quality specifications set by [ANP](https://www.gov.br/anp/),
Brazil's oil and fuels regulator, and appear as separate products at the pumps
and in price surveys.

## Why sulfur matters

When it burns, sulfur generates sulfur oxides and contributes to the emission
of particulate matter, pollutants that affect air quality and health. Sulfur
also harms the equipment that modern vehicles use to treat exhaust gases, such
as catalytic converters, particulate filters and the selective catalytic
reduction (SCR) system, which works with the reagent ARLA 32.

That is why the reduction of sulfur in diesel went hand in hand with the
stricter phases of **PROCONVE**, the Brazilian vehicle emissions control
program coordinated by [Ibama](https://www.gov.br/ibama/), the federal
environmental agency.

## What about ARLA 32?

Many trucks, buses and light commercial vehicles with an SCR system use, in
addition to diesel S10, **ARLA 32** (Agente Redutor Líquido Automotivo, an
automotive liquid reducing agent), a solution of urea in water that goes into
its own reservoir, separate from the fuel tank. In the exhaust, it reacts with
nitrogen oxides and reduces those emissions. Some precautions:

- never put ARLA 32 in the diesel tank, or diesel in the ARLA reservoir;
- buy from reliable suppliers and store it away from the sun, because heat and
  contamination degrade the solution;
- if the dashboard indicates a low level or a fault in the system, follow the
  manual: many vehicles limit power when the ARLA runs out or is out of
  specification.

ARLA 32 is not a fuel additive and does not replace diesel S10. In the
vehicles that require both, they work together.

## Who needs S10

With the arrival of the more demanding PROCONVE phases, diesel vehicles began
to leave the factory with aftertreatment systems that depend on diesel with a
low sulfur content. In general, this applies to diesel trucks, buses and light
commercial vehicles built from 2012 onward, but there are variations by
vehicle type and model year.

The safe way to know is to check:

- the **owner's manual**, which indicates the recommended fuel;
- the **sticker or marking** near the filler neck, which many manufacturers
  use to indicate "diesel S10 only";
- the dealership or the manufacturer, if in doubt.

## Can I use S10 in an older vehicle?

Yes. S10 is compatible with vehicles designed for S500. Besides the lower
sulfur content, the S10 specification is stricter in other parameters, such as
the cetane number, linked to ignition quality. The decision, in this case, is
mainly about cost: compare the prices of the two in your area before choosing.

## What happens if you use S500 in a vehicle that requires S10

Using diesel with more sulfur than intended can, over time, compromise the
catalytic converter, the particulate filter and the SCR system, increase
emissions and light up warnings on the dashboard. Repairs to these systems are
usually expensive, and using a fuel different from the recommended one can
affect the warranty.

If you filled up with the wrong diesel by mistake, seek guidance from the
dealership or a repair shop you trust, which can assess the case of your
vehicle.

## Diesel B: the blend with biodiesel

The diesel sold at stations is **diesel B**, which carries a mandatory
percentage of biodiesel set by the federal government. That percentage has
changed several times and may change again, so check the value in force in the
publications of [ANP](https://www.gov.br/anp/). The blend applies to both S10
and S500.

## How to identify it at the pump

The pump must clearly identify the product, as "Diesel S10" or "Diesel S500".
Before filling up, check the identification, especially at stations where the
two products sit side by side. If the vehicle belongs to someone else or to a
fleet, leave the manufacturer's recommendation written down for whoever
refuels it.

If the diesel seems out of the ordinary, with engine misfires or loss of power
right after filling up, see the guide
[adulterated fuel: how to identify and report it](/guias/combustivel-adulterado/)
(in Portuguese).

## Cost: compare in the same week

Because the two products have different specifications, the prices also tend
to be different. To compare with official data, use the
[reference prices by state and city](/precos/) (in Portuguese), which list S10
and S500 separately and with the collection date. To estimate the cost of a
route, the
[trip fuel cost calculator](/en/tools/trip-fuel-cost-calculator/) works with
any fuel sold by the liter.

The consumption of a diesel vehicle also depends a lot on the way you drive
and on maintenance. The tips in the guide
[how to save fuel](/guias/como-economizar-combustivel/) (in Portuguese) apply
to cars, pickups and trucks.

## The Brazilian context

Prices are per liter in Brazilian reais (BRL), and the price on the sign
already includes federal and state taxes. At most stations an attendant fills
the tank for you; you do not operate the pump yourself. Payment by card or by
Pix, Brazil's instant payment system, is common.
