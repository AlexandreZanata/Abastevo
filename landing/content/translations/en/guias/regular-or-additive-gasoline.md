::: resumo
**Quick answer:** regular gasoline and gasoline with additives meet the same basic ANP quality specification. Detergent and dispersant additives help limit deposits in the fuel system. Expect little direct change in power or fuel economy. Premium has a higher minimum octane and matters most for engines that call for it. Follow your owner's manual.
:::

## What gasoline sold in Brazil has in common

Brazilian stations sell gasoline C, a blend of gasoline and the federally mandated share of anhydrous ethanol. Regular, additive and premium gasoline must meet [ANP specifications](https://www.gov.br/anp/) for octane, ethanol, sulfur and other properties. ANP also monitors quality and carries out inspections. In-spec regular gasoline suits most gasoline and flex-fuel cars.

## What additives do

Detergents and dispersants help prevent residue on injectors, intake valves and in combustion chambers.

- Each distributor uses its own formula and brand name.
- The effect is preventive. Maintenance is still necessary, and a clogged injector needs attention.
- Any economy gain is indirect. A clean engine can run better, but a tank-to-tank increase in km/L is unlikely.

## What about premium?

Premium has higher minimum octane. Some high-compression and turbo engines call for it, and some premium grades also include additives. An engine designed for regular generally gains little in power or economy with premium. An engine that calls for premium may reduce ignition advance and lose performance if you fill with regular.

## What octane means

Octane measures gasoline's resistance to knock, which is uncontrolled combustion at the wrong time. Higher-compression engines may need more resistance to knock. Octane alone does not measure the fuel's energy or the engine's power.

## Where to find the fuel recommendation

Check the fueling or technical section of your owner's manual, the label inside the fuel door, or the manufacturer's website or customer service for the model and year. Pay attention to **recommended** versus **required**. If premium is recommended, regular may mean reduced performance. If premium is required, another fuel can risk damage or affect the warranty. If the manual calls for neither premium nor gasoline with additives, in-spec regular gasoline meets the engine's requirements.

## When gasoline with additives makes sense

Consider it when the manual recommends it, you want an extra preventive measure for the fuel system, or the car spends much of its time on short trips with frequent starts. Some drivers alternate additive and regular. The two are compatible in the same tank when both meet the gasoline C specification.

| Situation | Reasonable choice |
| --- | --- |
| Manual recommends regular | Regular, with additives optional as preventive care |
| Manual recommends gasoline with additives | Gasoline with additives |
| Turbo or high-compression engine calls for premium | Premium, as stated in the manual |
| Flex-fuel car choosing between ethanol and gasoline | Compare cost per kilometer before deciding |
: Fuel choice according to the vehicle manufacturer's recommendation

## Common myths

- **“Additives make a tank last much longer.”** Driving and maintenance generally matter much more. See [how to save fuel](/en/guides/save-fuel/).
- **“Additives clean any dirty engine.”** They help prevent deposits. Existing problems need diagnosis and maintenance.
- **“Premium makes every car more powerful.”** The engine must be designed to take advantage of it.
- **“Mixing regular and additive gasoline damages the engine.”** They are compatible.
