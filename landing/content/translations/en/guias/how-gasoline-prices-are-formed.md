::: resumo
**Quick answer:** the pump price of gasoline in Brazil is the sum of five
parts: the value of pure gasoline at the producer or importer, the federal
taxes (CIDE, PIS/Pasep and Cofins), the state tax ICMS, the cost of the
anhydrous ethanol blended in, and the margins and costs of distribution and
retail. The weight of each part changes over time and from state to state.
[Petrobras publishes an estimate of the breakdown](https://precos.petrobras.com.br/)
and ANP, Brazil's oil and fuels regulator,
[publishes the surveyed prices](https://www.gov.br/anp/).
:::

## Gasoline A and gasoline C: what you actually buy

The gasoline that leaves the refinery or arrives by import is called
**gasoline A**, with no ethanol. At the distribution terminals it receives
**anhydrous ethanol** (alcohol with practically no water) and becomes
**gasoline C**, the one sold at stations, both as regular gasoline and as
additive gasoline (gasolina aditivada).

The mandatory share of anhydrous ethanol in gasoline is set by the federal
government and has changed several times over the years. That is why this
guide does not fix a number: the value in force can be checked in the
publications of [ANP](https://www.gov.br/anp/). What matters for the price is
that part of every liter is ethanol, with a cost of its own.

## The parts of the price, one by one

### 1. Producer or importer

This is the value of the gasoline A sold by refineries and importers to
distributors. Petrobras is the largest supplier, but not the only one: there
are other refineries and companies that import fuel. Each supplier sets its
own commercial policy, which usually takes into account production costs, the
price of oil on the international market and the exchange rate.

When the press reports a "refinery price adjustment", this is the part it is
talking about. It does not reach the pump in full and at once, because the
other parts stay the same and stations are still selling the stock they bought
earlier.

### 2. Federal taxes

Three federal taxes apply to gasoline:

- **CIDE-Combustíveis**, created by
  [Law No. 10,336/2001](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/leis_2001/l10336.htm);
- **PIS/Pasep** and **Cofins**, social contributions.

These taxes are charged in a concentrated way at production and import, in
amounts set by the federal government. Over the years they have been reduced,
temporarily set to zero and reinstated by government decisions, which moves
the price directly. Official information is on the website of the
[Receita Federal](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/), the federal tax
authority.

### 3. State ICMS

ICMS is a tax levied by the states. Since 2023, for gasoline, it is charged
only once, at the origin, as a **fixed amount in reais per liter** that is the
same across the country, set by the states in
[CONFAZ](https://www.confaz.fazenda.gov.br/), the national council where the
states coordinate tax policy, on the basis of
[Complementary Law No. 192/2022](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/lcp/lcp192.htm).
Before that, each state applied a percentage to a reference price.

How this model works, why the fixed amount changes the tax's share of the
price and why hydrous ethanol (E100) was left out is explained in detail in
the guide [ICMS on fuels](/en/guides/icms-fuel-tax-explained/).

### 4. Anhydrous ethanol in the blend

Because gasoline C carries anhydrous ethanol, the price of that ethanol enters
the calculation. It depends on the sugarcane and corn harvests, on the demand
for sugar and ethanol, and on production and transport costs. Between
harvests, for example, this part can weigh more.

### 5. Distribution and retail

The last part covers everything that happens after the refinery: transport to
the terminals, storage, blending, freight to the station, wages, rent,
electricity, card fees, taxes on the business itself and the profit of
distributors and stations.

In Brazil, fuel prices are free: there is no official price table for
stations. Each distributor and each retailer sets its own price, within the
rules of competition and consumer protection. It is mainly this part that
explains why stations in the same city charge different amounts.

## How much each part weighs

There is no fixed split. The share of each part changes when the producer
adjusts its price, when the government changes taxes, when CONFAZ revises the
ICMS or when anhydrous ethanol becomes more or less expensive. It also changes
from state to state, because of freight and local margins.

So, instead of repeating percentages that soon go out of date, we point to
where to check:

| Part | Who sets it | Where to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gasoline A | Refineries and importers | Supplier announcements and [Petrobras's estimated breakdown](https://precos.petrobras.com.br/) |
| CIDE, PIS/Pasep and Cofins | Federal government | [Receita Federal](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/) |
| ICMS | States, in CONFAZ | [CONFAZ agreements](https://www.confaz.fazenda.gov.br/) |
| Anhydrous ethanol | Ethanol market | Publications of [ANP](https://www.gov.br/anp/) |
| Distribution and retail | Distributors and stations | Prices surveyed by [ANP](https://www.gov.br/anp/) |
: Parts of the gasoline price, who sets them and where to check the values in force

The Petrobras price composition page shows an estimate for the national
average and by state. It is useful for understanding the order of magnitude of
each part, but it is an estimate made by the company, not the real cost of a
specific station.

## Why the price changes from one week to the next

The price you see on the sign can change for several reasons, often combined:

- a producer price adjustment or a change in the cost of imports;
- a change in federal taxes or in the ICMS amount per liter;
- a rise or fall in anhydrous ethanol;
- changes in freight and local costs;
- competition between stations in the same area.

The pass-through is not instant. A station that bought a truckload of fuel
before an adjustment may take a few days to change its price, up or down. That
also explains why two neighboring stations react at different times. The guide
[why neighboring cities have different prices](/guias/precos-diferentes-entre-cidades/)
(in Portuguese) goes deeper into this point.

## How to follow the price with reliable data

The safest way to know whether the price in your area went up or down is to
compare the same source, in the same week, for the same fuel. On abastevo, the
[reference prices by state and city](/en/fuel-prices/) come from the
ANP weekly survey and always show the collection date.

Remember that this survey is a sample: it does not visit every station and
does not record the price right now. What it measures, and what it does not,
is explained in the [data methodology](/en/methodology/) and in
the guide
[how the ANP weekly survey works](/en/guides/anp-weekly-price-survey/).

If your question is which fuel to put in the tank, the
[ethanol or gasoline calculator](/en/tools/ethanol-or-gasoline-calculator/)
does the math with the prices you find.

## The Brazilian context

Prices are per liter in Brazilian reais (BRL), and the price on the sign
already includes federal and state taxes. At most stations an attendant fills
the tank for you; you do not operate the pump yourself. Payment by card or by
Pix, Brazil's instant payment system, is common.
