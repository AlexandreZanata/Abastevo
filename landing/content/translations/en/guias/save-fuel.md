::: resumo
**Quick answer:** drive smoothly and plan ahead, keep a moderate speed, check tire pressure and maintenance, remove unnecessary weight and compare fuels by **cost per kilometer**. Measure before and after changing a habit. Treat claims of dramatic savings from additives and gadgets with caution.
:::

## Measure before you change

Log a few tanks using the [full-tank method](/en/guides/measure-fuel-economy/), change one habit at a time and compare. The dashboard can show trends. The [fuel consumption calculator](/en/tools/fuel-consumption-calculator/) uses the amount you actually fill.

## Drive smoothly and anticipate stops

Hard acceleration and abrupt braking waste energy. Accelerate progressively and use the gear range indicated by the manual or display. Look ahead for traffic or red lights and ease off early. Many fuel-injected cars cut fuel during deceleration in gear. Leave enough following distance to adjust your speed smoothly.

## Control your highway speed

Air resistance rises roughly with the square of speed. A small increase at highway speeds can cost appreciably more fuel. Observe the speed limit and hold a steady speed when conditions allow, including with cruise control if available.

## Keep the tires properly inflated

Underinflation raises rolling resistance, fuel consumption and wear and can make driving less safe. Follow the vehicle's recommended pressure, usually in the manual or on a door or fuel flap label. Check cold tires at least monthly and before trips, including the spare.

## Keep up with maintenance

Replace air and fuel filters, spark plugs and oil as directed. Use the manufacturer's oil specification and keep up with wheel alignment and balancing. Have engine warning lights and misfires diagnosed, since a faulty sensor can increase fuel use.

## Reduce weight and drag

Remove unnecessary weight from the trunk and take off any roof racks or bicycle carriers you are not using. Extra weight matters more in stop-and-go traffic. Drag from roof accessories matters more on the highway.

## Air conditioning or open windows?

The air conditioner draws power. Open windows increase drag as speed rises. Open windows often use less fuel at low speeds. On the highway, closed windows with the air conditioner on are often better. The result depends on your car. Avoid running the air conditioner at maximum all the time.

## Limit unnecessary idling

Long waits with the engine on burn fuel while you are parked. Modern fuel-injected engines can normally warm up while you drive gently for the first few minutes.

## Choose fuel by cost per kilometer

The lowest price per liter may cost more per kilometer in a flex-fuel car, since ethanol usually gives fewer kilometers per liter than gasoline. Use the [ethanol or gasoline calculator](/en/tools/ethanol-or-gasoline-calculator/) with the prices you find. The [ANP price references by state and city](/en/fuel-prices/) show when observations were collected.

## Fueling advice and common myths

- Stop at the first automatic click. Topping up can damage the fuel vapor system.
- Fuel is stored in underground tanks. Their temperature changes little over the day, so filling overnight offers little practical benefit.
- Claims of large gains from additives and gadgets need independent evidence.
- On fuel-injected cars, coasting downhill in neutral reduces control and generally offers no fuel saving over decelerating in gear. Brazil's [Traffic Code](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9503compilado.htm) also prohibits driving downhill out of gear.

## Plan your routes

Combine errands, avoid heavy traffic where possible and choose routes with fewer stops. The [trip fuel cost calculator](/en/tools/trip-fuel-cost-calculator/) can help estimate travel costs.

## Compare your car with the official label

[Inmetro](https://www.gov.br/inmetro/) publishes standardized city and highway test results. Real consumption varies, but the label helps compare models and spot a large difference in your own readings.
