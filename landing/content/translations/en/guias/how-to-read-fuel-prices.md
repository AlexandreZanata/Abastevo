When you stop at a fuel station, you have probably noticed different amounts
for the same product or surprises at the moment of payment. Understanding how
Brazilian law regulates the display of prices and how to interpret the
information avoids improper charges and supports smarter decisions.

## Sign price vs. pump price

Article 20 of [ANP Resolution No. 948/2023](https://www.in.gov.br/web/dou/-/resolucao-anp-n-948-de-5-de-outubro-de-2023-515293933)
requires stations to post the upfront prices for all automotive fuels on the
sign at the entrance. The sign must be visible from a distance, day and night.
If deferred payment is available, those prices must also be on the sign.

Article 2 of [Brazilian Decree No. 10,634/2021](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2019-2022/2021/decreto/d10634.htm)
covers how regular and promotional prices are displayed. If a discount depends
on a loyalty app, the station must highlight the regular price and disclose
the promotional price and the discount.

If the price or payment term varies for the same fuel, Article 21 of the
resolution requires the pump or nozzle to identify the applicable terms and
show the total payable on those terms.

## Payment conditions (Law 13,455/2017)

In Brazil, Federal Law No. 13,455 authorizes businesses to charge different
prices depending on the payment term or the payment instrument used:

- **Upfront payment (cash or Pix, Brazil's instant payment system):** often
  used for the lowest price advertised on the sign.
- **Debit card:** generally aligned with the upfront price, but it may have
  its own rules if they are clearly informed before refueling.
- **Credit card:** may carry a legally permitted surcharge, as long as the
  sign shows the difference conspicuously.
- **Loyalty and cashback apps:** stations often advertise amounts with a
  discount exclusive to users of certain apps. That condition has to be
  clearly described so that it does not mislead the driver.

## Badges and the separation of sources on abastevo

To avoid confusion, the **abastevo** app adopts a strict separation between
data sources:

::: nota
**ANP reference:** identifies data obtained from the official survey of ANP,
Brazil's oil and fuels regulator. It comes with the date of the survey week
(e.g. "Week of September 20 to 26").

**Community:** an observation sent by a contributing driver who was at the
location. It indicates the exact date and time of the observation (e.g.
"Observed today at 14:30") and the payment condition recorded.
:::

## Branded station vs. unbranded station

Retail stations in Brazil fall into two main categories:

- **Branded stations:** display a distributor's brand. If they sell fuel from
  another supplier, they must display that supplier's trade name alongside the
  product identification (Article 20, paragraph 2).
- **Unbranded (independent) stations:** choose to operate without displaying a
  distributor's brand.

At either type of station, look on the liquid fuel pump for the supplying
distributor's CNPJ (company tax ID), legal name or trade name. The rule is in
Article 27 of [ANP Resolution No. 948/2023](https://www.in.gov.br/web/dou/-/resolucao-anp-n-948-de-5-de-outubro-de-2023-515293933).

## Consumer rights at the pump

If you suspect the quantity or the quality of the fuel you were sold, the
regulations of Inmetro, Brazil's national metrology institute, and of ANP give
the consumer the right to request immediate tests at the station:

- **Flow test (20-liter check):** the station must have a 20-liter standard
  measure verified and sealed by Inmetro. On request, the attendant must pour
  20 liters into the measure in the customer's presence. The maximum variation
  tolerated by law is only 100 ml (0.5%).
- **Graduated cylinder test (ethanol in gasoline):** in case of doubt about
  the proportion of ethanol in gasoline, the station is required to perform
  the graduated cylinder test with a water and salt solution to prove whether
  the anhydrous ethanol content is within the mandatory percentage in force,
  set by the federal government and published by
  [ANP](https://www.gov.br/anp/). That percentage changes from time to time,
  so we do not fix it here.

## In the app

check the source and the date of a price inside the app:

1. On a station's sheet on the map, "Price source" shows whether the value
   comes from "ANP" or from the "Community", with the survey week and the
   collection date.
2. In the "Station profile", "Community prices (beta)" and "ANP reference
   price" sit in separate cards.
3. The ANP card always carries the collection date and the week; the community
   card warns when there is no recent submission.

## The Brazilian context

Prices are per liter in Brazilian reais (BRL), and the price on the sign
already includes federal and state taxes. Regular gasoline is not pure
gasoline: it carries anhydrous ethanol. At most stations an attendant fills
the tank for you; you do not operate the pump yourself. Payment by Pix or card
is common.
