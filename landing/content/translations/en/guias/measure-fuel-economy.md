::: resumo
**Quick answer:** fill the tank until the pump first stops, reset the trip meter and drive as usual. At the next fill-up, stop at the first click again. Divide the distance on the trip meter by the liters added at that second stop. Repeat across several tanks. The [fuel consumption calculator](/en/tools/fuel-consumption-calculator/) also gives you the cost per kilometer.
:::

## The formula

```
fuel economy (km/L) = distance driven (km) ÷ liters added
```

The second fill-up replaces the fuel you used over the measured distance, provided the starting and ending levels were the same. This is the full-tank, or tank-to-tank, method.

## The full-tank method, step by step

1. Fill until the pump first clicks off. Avoid topping up.
2. Reset the trip meter or write down the odometer reading.
3. Drive normally. Longer distances give a more reliable reading, so aim to drive until you are close to the reserve.
4. Refill to the first click, preferably at the same pump and station.
5. Record the pump's liters and the distance on the trip meter.
6. Divide the distance by the liters.

## Worked example

You drive 412 km and add 35.6 liters to fill the tank. The calculation is 412 ÷ 35.6 = **11.6 km/L**. At an illustrative R$ 6.29 per liter, the cost is about R$ 0.54 per kilometer and R$ 223.92 for the fill-up.

## What can distort a reading

- **Topping up** changes the fuel level between measurements and can damage the vapor system.
- **Different pumps or slopes** can change when the pump stops. Use the same pump and level spot when possible.
- **Short distances** magnify small differences in fuel level.
- **A single measurement** reflects that week's traffic, load and weather. Take three or four readings.
- **Different driving conditions** affect results. Label city, highway and mixed use.
- **Nonstandard wheel or tire sizes** can affect the odometer.

## Record the readings and calculate the combined average

Keep a simple log on paper or on your phone to see your car's usual fuel economy and notice when it changes.

Add the distances and the liters before dividing. In the table, 1,387 km ÷ 108.7 liters gives **12.8 km/L**. Averaging the three ratios directly would give each measurement the same weight. If your economy drops suddenly in similar driving conditions, check tire pressure, maintenance and the quality of the most recent fill-up.

## Kilometers per liter or liters per 100 km?

Brazil commonly uses km/L. Other countries and some vehicle specifications use liters per 100 km.

```
liters per 100 km = 100 ÷ economy in km/L
km/L = 100 ÷ liters per 100 km
```

In the worked example, 11.6 km/L is about 8.6 liters per 100 km.

## What about the trip computer?

The dashboard's estimate is useful for following trends. For decisions such as choosing between ethanol and gasoline, use the fuel actually dispensed at the pump.

## Compare ethanol and gasoline in your car

Measure both fuels under similar conditions. Divide ethanol economy by gasoline economy.

```
your car's break-even ratio = ethanol km/L ÷ gasoline km/L
```

At 8.7 km/L on ethanol and 12.5 km/L on gasoline, the ratio is 69.6%. Ethanol is economical at up to 69.6% of the gasoline price. Your measurement replaces the general 70% rule. Use the [ethanol or gasoline calculator](/en/tools/ethanol-or-gasoline-calculator/) with the prices you find, and see the [full guide to the rule](/en/guides/ethanol-or-gasoline/).

## Use the official label as a reference

[Inmetro](https://www.gov.br/inmetro/) publishes city and highway laboratory results through Brazil's vehicle labeling program. Traffic, terrain, load and driving affect real results. If your reading falls far below the label, check maintenance and tire pressure.

## From fuel economy to cost per kilometer

```
cost per km = price per liter ÷ economy (km/L)
```

Use this result to plan a monthly budget, [estimate a trip](/en/tools/trip-fuel-cost-calculator/) and [compare cost per kilometer](/en/tools/cost-per-km-calculator/). The [fuel-saving guide](/en/guides/save-fuel/) covers habits that can help.
