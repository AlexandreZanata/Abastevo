::: resumo
**Quick answer:** compressed natural gas (CNG, called GNV in Brazil) often costs less per kilometer than gasoline or ethanol. Conversion is more likely to pay off if you drive a lot, have CNG stations on your usual routes, accept the loss of trunk space and some performance, and complete installation by an [Inmetro-registered installer](https://www.gov.br/inmetro/), safety inspection and vehicle document updates.
:::

## How CNG is sold

Stations sell CNG by the cubic meter (m³). Compare the cost of driving one kilometer on each fuel. Fuel economy in km/m³ depends on the car, kit, tuning and driving conditions. If the kit is not installed yet, ask the installer for an estimate for your model.

## Calculate the cost per kilometer

```
CNG cost per km = price per m³ ÷ economy (km/m³)
gasoline or ethanol cost per km = price per liter ÷ economy (km/L)
```

Illustrative example: at R$ 4.80 per m³ and 12 km/m³, CNG costs R$ 0.40 per kilometer. At R$ 6.29 per liter and 11 km/L, gasoline costs R$ 0.572 per kilometer. The difference is R$ 0.172 per kilometer. Use the [fuel consumption calculator](/en/tools/fuel-consumption-calculator/) to measure your current fuel and the [cost per kilometer calculator](/en/tools/cost-per-km-calculator/) to compare.

## Calculate the payback period

The initial cost includes the kit, cylinder, labor, inspection and registration.

```
monthly savings = km per month × saving per km
months to pay back = total installation cost ÷ monthly savings
```

In the same illustrative example, 2,000 km per month at R$ 0.172 savings per kilometer saves R$ 344 per month. At a hypothetical R$ 5,000 installation cost, payback is about 15 months. Get real quotes and use your own figures. Low mileage can mean years of payback, during which relative fuel prices may change.

## Costs and checks before deciding

- Use an Inmetro-registered installer and certified components. An improperly installed kit puts safety at risk and cannot be registered.
- **Inspection and registration:** after installation, an accredited body inspects the vehicle and its registration must reflect the modification. Check current and periodic requirements with [Inmetro](https://www.gov.br/inmetro/) and your state's Detran.
- **Space and weight:** the cylinder takes up trunk space and adds weight.
- **Performance:** the car may lose some power while running on gas.
- **Liquid fuel:** some kits still use gasoline or ethanol at times, such as startup.
- **Warranty:** check the vehicle maker's terms.
- **Tax:** some states offer different IPVA terms for gas-powered vehicles. Verify the rule with your state's finance department before including it in your calculation.

## Everyday safety

- Follow the station's instructions, switch off the engine and leave the car if instructed.
- Natural gas is odorized so leaks can be detected. If you smell gas, stop in a ventilated place, switch off the vehicle, close the cylinder valve only if you know how, and contact a registered installer.
- Observe the cylinder's required requalification date. Avoid expired, dented or unidentified cylinders.
- Follow the kit maintenance intervals. Incorrect tuning can increase consumption and cause engine trouble.
- Keep inspections and registration current. An irregular conversion can result in the vehicle being detained during a traffic inspection.

## Station availability

Check CNG availability on everyday and travel routes before installing a kit. Detours to refuel reduce the savings. The [ANP survey prices by state and city](/en/fuel-prices/) show dated values per m³ when there is a sample.

## Who tends to save the most?

Drivers with high mileage, including taxi and ride-hailing drivers or small fleets, can recover the cost faster where CNG is available and the savings per kilometer are substantial. If you drive little or CNG is scarce, see the [fuel-saving guide](/en/guides/save-fuel/).
