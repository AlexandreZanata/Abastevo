## Why the 70% rule works

Hydrous ethanol (E100) carries less energy per liter than gasoline. One liter
of Brazilian gasoline — gasoline C, pure gasoline blended with the mandatory
share of anhydrous ethanol set by the federal government and published by ANP —
releases about 31 to 32 MJ per liter. One liter of hydrous ethanol releases
about 22 to 23 MJ per liter.

That puts the energy of ethanol at roughly **70%** of the energy in the same
volume of gasoline. To travel the same distance, the engine injects more
ethanol.

## The math at the pump

Divide the ethanol price by the gasoline price. If the result is **0.70 or
less**, ethanol tends to cost less per kilometer. If it is **above 0.70**,
gasoline tends to cost less.

| Gasoline price | Ethanol pays off up to 70% | Ethanol at a modern 73% parity |
|---|---|---|
| R$ 5.00 | R$ 3.50 | R$ 3.65 |
| R$ 5.50 | R$ 3.85 | R$ 4.01 |
| R$ 6.00 | R$ 4.20 | R$ 4.38 |
| R$ 6.50 | R$ 4.55 | R$ 4.74 |
: Illustrative parity values, not prices from any station or week.

## When the rule changes

The 70% mark is a classic engineering estimate, but the real ratio depends on
the engine:

- **Modern flex engines with direct injection and turbo:** high compression and
  electronic management take advantage of ethanol's high octane rating (above
  100 RON), so the real parity can reach **72% to 75%**.
- **Older engines or severe urban use:** frequent cold starts and short trips in
  heavy traffic can drop ethanol's efficiency to **66% to 68%** of gasoline.

## Measure your own car

Fill the tank with gasoline, reset the trip odometer and drive to the reserve.
Divide the kilometers driven by the liters added to get km/l on gasoline. Fill
the tank with ethanol next time and repeat. Divide the ethanol km/l by the
gasoline km/l: that fraction is your car's exact parity.

## The Brazilian context

Prices are per liter in Brazilian reais (BRL), and the price on the sign
already includes federal and state taxes. Regular gasoline is not pure
gasoline: it carries anhydrous ethanol, so the ethanol you buy at the pump is
not the same product that is blended into gasoline. At most stations an
attendant fills the tank for you; you do not operate the pump yourself. Payment
by Pix or card is common.
