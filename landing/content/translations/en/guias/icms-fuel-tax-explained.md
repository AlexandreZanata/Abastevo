::: resumo
**Quick answer:** since 2023, the ICMS, a Brazilian state tax, on gasoline
(and on the anhydrous ethanol blended into it), on diesel and biodiesel, and
on cooking gas (LPG) is charged **only once**, at production or import, as a
**fixed amount in reais per liter** (or per kilogram, in the case of LPG), the
same across the country. This model was created by
[Complementary Law No. 192/2022](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/lcp/lcp192.htm)
and the amounts are set by the states in
[CONFAZ](https://www.confaz.fazenda.gov.br/), the national council where the
states coordinate tax policy. Hydrous ethanol (E100), sold pure at the pump,
was left out of this model.
:::

## What ICMS is and why it weighs on fuel

The Imposto sobre Circulação de Mercadorias e Serviços (ICMS), a tax on the
circulation of goods and services, is a state tax. It is one of the main
sources of revenue for the states, and fuels are among the products that raise
the most. That is why any change in the way ICMS is charged on fuels moves the
pump price and public finances at the same time.

ICMS is one of the five parts of the price, alongside the producer value, the
federal taxes, the anhydrous ethanol and the distribution and retail margins.
The full picture is in the guide
[how gasoline prices are formed](/en/guides/how-gasoline-prices-are-formed/).

## How it was before: a percentage of a reference price

Until 2023, each state applied a **percentage rate** (the so-called *ad
valorem* model) to a reference price estimated by its own finance department
(secretaria da fazenda), known as the weighted average price to the final
consumer. The tax was collected in advance, at the start of the chain,
through tax substitution (substituição tributária).

This model had two important consequences:

- the rates and the reference prices were different in each state, so the tax
  per liter also varied a lot from state to state;
- when the fuel price went up, the tax amount went up with it, because the
  percentage was applied to a higher price.

In 2022,
[Complementary Law No. 194/2022](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/lcp/lcp194.htm)
began to treat fuels as essential goods, which limited the percentage rates
the states could apply. The current model came into force after that.

## How it is now: a single charge and a fixed amount per liter

Complementary Law No. 192/2022 determined that the ICMS on some fuels would
become **single-phase** (charged only once in the whole chain) and have a
**specific rate** (a fixed amount per unit of measure, called the *ad rem*
model). The products included are:

- gasoline and fuel-grade anhydrous ethanol;
- diesel and biodiesel;
- liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), including the one derived from natural gas.

Under the law, the amounts are **uniform throughout the national territory**,
may be different for each product, and are set by decision of the states in
CONFAZ, through agreements (convênios). The new model took effect in 2023,
first for diesel and LPG and then for gasoline.

In practice, this means that the ICMS on a liter of gasoline is the same in
any state, regardless of whether the liter costs more or less at the pump. The
tax is collected by the producer or importer, and the following stages of the
chain do not pay ICMS again on that product.

## How the math works in practice

With a fixed amount per liter, the share of ICMS in the final price comes to
depend on the pump price: the more expensive the liter, the smaller the tax's
slice, and vice versa. The example below uses a **hypothetical** amount of
R$ 1.50 per liter, chosen only to make the math easier. It is not the amount
in force.

| Pump price (example) | Hypothetical fixed ICMS | Share of the price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R$ 5.00 | R$ 1.50 | 30% |
| R$ 6.00 | R$ 1.50 | 25% |
| R$ 7.50 | R$ 1.50 | 20% |
: Hypothetical example: how a fixed ICMS per liter changes weight with the pump price

Two practical consequences:

1. An adjustment in the producer price no longer raises the ICMS
   automatically. The tax per liter only changes when CONFAZ approves a new
   amount.
2. When CONFAZ adjusts the amount per liter, all states start charging the new
   amount at the same time, and the effect shows up in the price in a similar
   way across the whole country.

## What about hydrous ethanol?

Hydrous ethanol, the one you put pure into the tank of a flex-fuel car, **is
not on the list** in Complementary Law No. 192/2022. It is still taxed under
the percentage model, with rates set by each state. That is why the ICMS on
ethanol still varies between states, and that difference helps explain why
ethanol pays off more in some places than in others.

If you want to know whether switching fuels is worth it in your city, the
[ethanol or gasoline calculator](/en/tools/ethanol-or-gasoline-calculator/)
does the math with local prices.

CNG (compressed natural gas for vehicles, called GNV in Brazil) is also not
among the products in the law and follows the rules of each state. To check
how these products are taxed, consult your state's finance department.

## Where to check the amount in force

The ICMS amount per liter changes by decision of the states, so we do not fix
numbers in this guide. To find the current amount:

- consult the agreements published by
  [CONFAZ](https://www.confaz.fazenda.gov.br/), which give the amounts per
  product and the date they take effect;
- see the
  [Petrobras price composition page](https://precos.petrobras.com.br/), which
  shows an estimate of the ICMS part in the average price by state;
- for hydrous ethanol and CNG, look for your state's finance department.

## What the fixed ICMS does not explain

Even with the same ICMS across the whole country for gasoline and diesel, the
pump price still differs between states, cities and stations. Freight,
competition, local costs and stock keep weighing, as shown in the guide
[why neighboring cities have different prices](/guias/precos-diferentes-entre-cidades/)
(in Portuguese).

To follow the effect of these differences with official data, check the
[reference prices by state](/en/fuel-prices/), always with the
collection date, and understand the limits of that sample in the
[data methodology](/en/methodology/).

## The Brazilian context

Prices are per liter in Brazilian reais (BRL), and the price on the sign
already includes federal and state taxes. Brazilian gasoline is a blend:
gasoline C contains the mandatory share of anhydrous ethanol set by the
federal government.

The abastevo Android app is in development. The captures that illustrate this
section are on the Portuguese page: they are real, were taken on November 17,
2026, and show the reference of ANP, Brazil's oil and fuels regulator, for the
week indicated, not the pump price. The app does not separate ICMS from the

   shows the final consumer price, with all the parts already in it.
