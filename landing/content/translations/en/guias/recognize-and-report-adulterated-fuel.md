::: resumo
**Quick answer:** misfires, power loss, increased consumption or a difficult start after refueling can signal off-specification fuel. Ask the station about basic quality tests and keep the receipt and pictures of the pump. Report suspected quality problems to [ANP](https://www.gov.br/anp/). Contact Procon or [consumidor.gov.br](https://www.consumidor.gov.br/) for a consumer claim.
:::

## What off-specification fuel means

[ANP](https://www.gov.br/anp/) sets quality limits for each fuel. Fuel can fall outside them through deliberate adulteration, contamination or improper storage. Examples include gasoline with excess anhydrous ethanol or unauthorized solvents, hydrous ethanol with too much water, and diesel contamination or sulfur content that differs from the grade indicated on the pump.

## Signs in the car

Watch for stuttering, misfires, knock, power loss on hills or while accelerating, unusual fuel consumption, difficulty starting or an engine warning light shortly after filling up. Mechanical faults can cause the same symptoms. A workshop's diagnosis can help distinguish the cause and provide evidence if it points to the fuel.

## Tests you can ask the station to perform

The ANP rules require stations to have equipment for basic tests and carry them out on request. A graduated cylinder test uses a water and salt solution to separate ethanol from gasoline. The change in volume indicates the proportion of ethanol for comparison with the current mandate. A hydrous ethanol pump has a visible temperature and density gauge. Ask the attendant to show its reading and the reference table. Check [ANP's current rules](https://www.gov.br/anp/) for required tests.

## Keep the evidence

- Ask for the tax receipt with the date, time, station, CNPJ, fuel and quantity. See the [consumer rights guide](/en/guides/fuel-station-consumer-rights/).
- Photograph the pump, fuel identification and price sign.
- Keep a workshop report that identifies fuel as the possible cause, plus quotes or repair receipts.
- Record the date, time, mileage and symptoms.

## What to do after noticing a problem

1. Limit driving if the engine is running badly.
2. Use a different station until the issue is understood.
3. Ask for the station tests if you are nearby and note the results.
4. Get a written diagnosis from a trusted workshop.
5. If the workshop drains the tank, ask whether it can keep a sealed sample marked with the date and station.
6. Collect the receipt, photos, report and notes before making your complaint.

## Report to ANP

Use the consumer channels listed on the [ANP website](https://www.gov.br/anp/), such as the contact form or consumer relations phone line. Include the station's name, business registration number (CNPJ) and address, the refueling date and time, the fuel and quantity, what happened and the evidence you have. ANP can investigate and penalize violations. The [ANP enforcement pages](/fiscalizacao/) in Portuguese show published fines and the source and limitations of those records.

## Compensation through consumer channels

ANP handles enforcement. For compensation for fuel or repairs, consult your local or state Procon, [consumidor.gov.br](https://www.consumidor.gov.br/) for participating businesses, or the small claims court for eligible amounts. The [Brazilian Consumer Protection Code](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8078compilado.htm) covers compensation for damage from defective products.

## A pump delivering too little: the 20-liter measure

Volume is a separate issue. Ask the station to check the pump with its **20-liter standard measure** verified through [Inmetro](https://www.gov.br/inmetro/). The attendant fills the measure and compares the volume to the pump display. Report discrepancies above the permitted tolerance to Inmetro or your state's Ipem.

## How to reduce risk

Choose stations you know. Compare unusually low prices with the same week's dated [ANP reference](/en/fuel-prices/). Price alone does not establish quality. Check the fuel and distributor identification as described in the [guide to reading prices](/en/guides/how-to-read-fuel-prices/) and use the grade your vehicle maker recommends. See [regular, additive and premium gasoline](/en/guides/regular-or-additive-gasoline/).
