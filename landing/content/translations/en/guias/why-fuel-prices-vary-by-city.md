::: resumo
Fuel prices are freely set in Brazil. Each station sets its own. The difference between nearby cities often comes down to how far fuel travels from the distribution terminal, the level of competition, the station's local costs, when it bought its stock and state taxes on some fuels. The number of stations surveyed in each city also affects the ANP figures.
:::

## Each station sets its own price

There is no official retail price list. Refineries and importers sell to distributors, who sell to stations. Each sets its own price within competition and consumer protection rules. Two stations on the same street can charge different prices, just as prices can vary between neighboring cities.

The [guide to how gasoline prices are formed](/en/guides/how-gasoline-prices-are-formed/) covers the producer's price, taxes, anhydrous ethanol, distribution and retail. The factors below help explain why prices differ between nearby cities.

## Distance from the terminal and transport costs

Refineries and import terminals send fuel to **distribution terminals**, where it is stored and blended. Trucks then deliver it to stations. The farther a station is from the terminal, the more it costs to deliver the fuel, raising the cost per liter.

Two neighboring cities may use different terminals. One may be on the usual delivery route, while the other requires a detour. Delivery costs tend to account for a larger share of the retail price in areas farther from terminals.

## Competition and sales volume

Larger cities and cities along busy highways tend to have more stations competing for the same drivers. That competition tends to push margins down. Small towns with few stations may see less price competition.

Sales volume also matters. A station that sells more fuel spreads its fixed costs over more liters.

## Local operating costs

Land rent, wages, electricity, security, maintenance, card fees and business taxes vary by city. A station in an area with higher property costs usually pays more to operate, and some of that cost shows up in the fuel price.

## Branded and independent stations

Branded stations have a contract with a distributor and buy fuel from it. Independent stations, called *bandeira branca* in Brazil, can buy from different authorized distributors. Terms vary across contracts and can affect what drivers pay. The [guide to reading prices at the pump and on the sign](/en/guides/how-to-read-fuel-prices/) explains how to identify the fuel supplier at the pump.

## Inventory and when prices change

Stations buy fuel in truckloads and sell it over several days. After a distributor changes its price, a station that just took delivery may take time to pass along the increase or decrease. Right after an adjustment, nearby cities, and even neighboring stations, may change their prices at different times.

## Taxes that still vary by state

Since 2023, the ICMS on gasoline and diesel has been a fixed amount per liter across Brazil. It no longer explains interstate differences for those fuels. States still set the tax on hydrous ethanol and compressed natural gas (CNG). The ethanol price may reflect that difference when neighboring cities sit on opposite sides of a state border. See the [guide to Brazil's ICMS fuel tax](/en/guides/icms-fuel-tax-explained/) for details.

## Differences in the ANP survey sample

The ANP's weekly survey is a **sample**. The agency surveys selected stations in selected municipalities. Keep that coverage in mind when comparing cities.

- An average based on only a few stations can fluctuate more from week to week.
- Some cities are surveyed only in certain weeks. Others are outside the survey.
- Averages from different weeks reflect different points in time.

Compare **the same source, week and fuel**, and check how many stations the sample includes. Abastevo's [reference prices by state and city](/en/fuel-prices/) show when the data was collected. The [data methodology](/en/methodology/) explains how that sample is handled. See the [guide to the ANP's weekly survey](/en/guides/anp-weekly-price-survey/) for more context.

## Is it worth filling up in the next city?

It depends on the saving per liter, how much fuel you buy and what it costs to drive there and back.

```
savings = liters purchased × price difference per liter
round-trip fuel cost = round-trip distance (km) ÷ fuel economy (km/L) × price per liter
```

With illustrative prices, buying 40 liters at R$ 0.20 less per liter saves R$ 8.00. If the round trip is 30 km, your car gets 12 km/L and fuel costs R$ 6.00 per liter, the trip uses 2.5 liters and costs R$ 15.00. That outweighs the saving.

The calculation changes if you are already passing through the city for another reason. For longer routes, use the [trip fuel cost calculator](/en/tools/trip-fuel-cost-calculator/).

## What to check before drawing a conclusion

- The reference price reflects the week surveyed. Check the station for today's pump price.
- Differences of a few cents may disappear from one week to the next.
- Payment terms matter. The cash and card prices can differ, and the sign must show the difference.
